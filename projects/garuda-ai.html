Garuda AI: Intelligent Enterprise Insider Threat Detection

A machine learning-driven security platform designed to identify malicious internal activity, unauthorized privilege escalation, and abnormal data exfiltration using historical behavioral baselines.

1. The Problem

Traditional perimeter firewalls and endpoint antivirus products guard against external intruders, but they remain essentially blind to threats from verified credentials. An authorized employee or an attacker operating with compromised corporate credentials can silently query confidential data stores, copy files to unmonitored endpoints, or escalate privileges without triggering simple rule-based alarms.

Enterprise SOC (Security Operations Center) analysts drown in thousands of daily alerts, creating fatigue and allowing sophisticated insider attacks to go undetected for months.

2. Intended Users & Deployment Scope

SOC Security Analysts

Requires contextualized threat vectors rather than raw log dumps, highlighting deviations with explainable feature importance.

Chief Information Security Officers (CISOs)

Needs a high-level posture dashboard revealing organization-wide risk hotspots and departments with anomalous access trends.

Compliance & Data Audit Teams

Requires auditable traces when sensitive personal records or intellectual property repositories are accessed outside operating hours.

3. Proposed & Implemented Solution

Garuda AI implements User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA). It ingests system audit logs, normalizes heterogeneous log formats, aggregates activities into chronological user session vectors, and feeds them into statistical anomaly algorithms to detect high-entropy deviation from historic baseline behavior.

5. Engineering Challenges & Trade-offs

Extreme Class Imbalance

Legitimate system actions account for >99.9% of all corporate logs. Standard supervised classifiers fail drastically. Switched to unsupervised density estimation and anomaly scoring to isolate true outliers without over-alerting.

Legitimate Work Habits vs Malicious Shifts

Engineers often pull late-night shifts before major releases. Hardcoded time thresholds generated severe false alarms. Implemented rolling 14-day individual baselines to accommodate natural variations.
