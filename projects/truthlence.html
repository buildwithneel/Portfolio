Truthlence: Real-Time AI Deepfake Detection & Media Verification

An AI-powered video integrity platform engineered to expose facial manipulation, synthetic lip-sync anomalies, and generative artifacts in digital video streams before misinformation propagates.

1. The Problem

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) and diffusion models have made hyper-realistic facial reenactment and voice synthesis widely accessible. In political elections, corporate announcements, and digital KYC onboarding, malicious actors exploit deepfake videos to deceive viewers with near-zero technical barrier to entry.

Existing commercial verification tools are typically proprietary enterprise black-boxes that require heavy offline cloud computation, rendering them impractical for fast-moving editorial desks, verification workflows, and edge applications.

2. Target Users & Use Cases

Digital Journalists & Fact-Checkers

Needs rapid, frame-level scrutiny of viral social media video clips before publishing verification reports.

Identity Verification & Fintech KYC

Mitigates injection attacks where pre-recorded synthetic faces bypass automated video liveness checks.

Security Research Teams

Requires transparent explainability heatmaps pinpointing exactly which facial regions show synthetic artifacts.

3. Proposed & Implemented Solution

Truthlence processes video inputs through a staged multi-modal vision pipeline. Rather than analyzing the whole frame at once, the system isolates facial bounding regions, tracks landmark stability across temporal sequences, and analyzes high-frequency spectral artifacts generated by convolutional upsamplers.

4. Neel's Role & Implementation Decisions

As the lead creator behind the project concept and prototype:

  • Formulated the modular video pipeline architecture separating video extraction, landmark tracking, and inference workers.
  • Implemented face detection and bounding region normalizations using OpenCV and MediaPipe to ensure consistent pixel density.
  • Investigated Fourier frequency transform (FFT) analysis to identify synthetic checkerboard artifacts typical of generative neural networks.
  • Designed human-interpretable output telemetry: confidence percentage, frame anomaly timeline, and suspicious region highlighting.

5. Challenges & Engineering Trade-offs

Compression Artifacts vs Synthetic Glitches

Heavy H.264/H.265 compression on platforms like WhatsApp or Telegram degrades high-frequency pixel details, creating false-positive signals in naive models. Mitigated by training with varying compression qualities.

Frame-by-Frame vs Temporal Smoothing

Single-frame inference produces noisy confidence scores across a 30fps clip. Implemented a sliding temporal window of 15 frames to smooth predictions and track optical flow continuity.

6. Current Status & Substantiated Evidence

Honest Status Disclosure

Truthlence is currently in active prototyping. The underlying vision extraction pipelines and initial synthetic detection experiments have been validated in offline Jupyter/Python test scripts. Full real-time web deployment is ongoing as part of current research in 2026.

Future Roadmap:

  1. Cross-modal audio-visual synchronization to catch synthetic voice desync (lip-sync discrepancy modeling).
  2. Browser-based WebAssembly inference client for lightweight client-side media scanning without server uploads.
  3. Public testing harness on open benchmark deepfake datasets (FaceForensics++, Celeb-DF).
